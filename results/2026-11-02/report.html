Full-context KV quantization: agentic workflows, zero–20 key scores

W2: q4_0 worse on 0/2 paired arms, ties on the rest; W2 had no scored instance on gemma4-26b-a4b__f16, gemma4-26b-a4b__q4_0; W3 retention: q4_0 worse on 0/2 paired arms; W4: 0/36 ledger reconciliations reached the expected hash; W5 answer keys fell on ornith-35b__q4_0 (12/18); W1 mean KLD q4_0: gemma4-26b-a4b=0.20515, ornith-35b=0.03125, qwen36-35b=0.00951; 64 loop-halted instance(s), 12 context-full halt(s) over 6 arms.

Compressing the cache to q4_0 changed what all three models predicted, but most task probes could not turn that into a measurable loss. Against each model's own f16 run, mean KLD was 0.2052 on Gemma, 0.0313 on Ornith and 0.0095 on Qwen3.6, and same-top agreement fell to 81.2%, 88.9% and 90.7%. Deep-context perplexity moved by +3.1%, -3.8% and +1.1%, so the effect is model-specific rather than a uniform penalty.

The counted workflows mostly saturated or failed for reasons unrelated to the cache. Ornith and Qwen3.6 scored 20/20 on both the 20-key retrieval and the compaction brief in both cache types. Gemma produced no scored 20-key answer in either arm because it looped in reasoning, and both arms failed the same way.

No arm reconciled the ledger (0/36 attempts), and Qwen3.6 wrote all six manual sections in both arms. The one graded loss that tracks the cache is Ornith's search arm: q4_0 answered 12/18 keys against 18/18 at f16 and made six searches instead of nine, skipping a hop.

The distribution shift is real and largest on Gemma, but the study can attribute a task-level loss to q4_0 only on that search probe; it cannot say compression made the other workflows worse.

This page compares an uncompressed 16-bit key/value cache (f16) with a 4-bit cache (q4_0) inside three models, at one full-context rung each. KV is the key/value cache, the stored attention state for every token the model has already read. KLD is Kullback–Leibler divergence and PPL is perplexity. The workflow labels W1–W6 mean the distribution instrument, the 20-key retrieval probe, compaction, the ledger, agentic search and long-form writing, in that order.

Premise, power and disclosure

The list below states the sample sizes and rules that apply to every table on the page; read it before comparing arms.

  • Instances per arm: W2 n=12; smallest attainable p=0.0005.
  • Instances per arm: W3 n=12; smallest attainable p=0.0005.
  • Instances per arm: W4 n=6; smallest attainable p=0.0312.
  • Instances per arm: W5 n=6; smallest attainable p=0.0312.
  • Instances per arm: W6 n=6; smallest attainable p=0.0312.
  • One greedy draw per instance: temperature: 0.0, no other sampling key, no seeds, no reps. Greedy is not a production setting; every loop count is about greedy decoding.
  • No reasoning budget, no retry path. The only cap is the 64k response ceiling, recorded as ceiling.
  • Pairwise rule: paired difference against the same model's f16 arm, cluster bootstrap over instances (1,000 resamples, seeded); worse only when the interval excludes 0 and the point estimate is worse.

Decision rule

An arm is worse if the lower bound of its paired interval excludes 0 and its point estimate is worse than that model's f16 arm; ties otherwise. Behaviour counts are reported with intervals and are never the basis of a ranking on their own.
